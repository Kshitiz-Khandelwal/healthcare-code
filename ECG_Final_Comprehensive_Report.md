## 1. Executive Summary

This project presents an end-to-end diagnostic framework that automatically ingests raw 12-lead ECG records, extracts both handcrafted clinical heart rate variability (HRV) metrics and deep convolutional neural network (CNN) embeddings, and classifies cardiac rhythms into **Normal, Arrhythmia, or Other / Unknown** classes.

Traditional ECG analysis relies on human measurement of intervals (PR, QRS, QT) and statistical HRV. Deep learning models extract rich, spatial representation but suffer from a "black-box" lack of clinical interpretability. This project introduces a **hybrid feature-fusion approach** that leverages both:
- **17 Handcrafted Features**: Extracted using signal-processing techniques to capture temporal rhythm variability and heartbeat morphology.
- **1792 Deep CNN Features**: Generated by converting ECG signals into 2D continuous wavelet transform (CWT) scalogram images and extracting features using a pre-trained **EfficientNet-B4** backbone.

The fused feature vector is compressed using PCA and fed into an optimized **LightGBM** classifier. The pipeline is deployed as a live interactive dashboard on **Vercel** ([Live Dashboard Link](https://healthcare-code-2azi-lime.vercel.app/)), backed by rule-based risk levels and natural language explanations.

## 4. Signal Preprocessing Pipeline

ECG signals are susceptible to environmental noise and physiological artifacts. Raw signals undergo two primary cleaning operations before feature extraction:

### 1. 4th-Order Butterworth Bandpass Filter (0.5 Hz - 40 Hz)
- **Low-frequency Cutoff (0.5 Hz)**: Filters out **baseline wander**, which is slow drifting of the signal caused by patient breathing, skin-electrode impedance changes, or body movement.
- **High-frequency Cutoff (40 Hz)**: Filters out **powerline interference** (50/60 Hz hum from electrical outlets) and high-frequency electromyographic (EMG) muscle noise.
- **Implementation**: Applied using `scipy.signal.filtfilt` (zero-phase filtering). This performs both forward and backward passes over the signal, which results in zero phase distortion, keeping the physical alignment of waves (P, Q, R, S, T) mathematically intact.

### 2. Z-Score Normalization
Every filtered signal lead is scaled to have a mean of zero and a standard deviation of 1:
$$\hat{x} = \frac{x - \mu}{\sigma}$$
This centers all patients on the same amplitude scale, neutralizing variations caused by electrode placement, skin thickness, or amplifier settings, and ensuring consistent feature extraction.

### 3. Lead Selection
The standard 12 leads are subsetted to three diagnostic leads:
*   **Lead I**: Captures the lateral electrical axis (left-to-right).
*   **Lead II**: The gold standard for rhythm analysis, aligning directly with the heart's primary electrical vector (Sinoatrial node to ventricles), providing the cleanest P-wave and R-peak spikes.
*   **Lead V5**: A precordial lead capturing left ventricular anterior/lateral wall activity.

## 5. Feature Extraction Branch A: Handcrafted Clinical Features

A set of 17 temporal and statistical features are extracted on the fly from Lead II (the rhythm standard) using custom signal-processing scripts:

### Peak Detection Parameters
Heartbeats (R-peaks) are localized using `scipy.signal.find_peaks` on the normalized Lead II signal:
- `distance = 150 ms` (enforces a physiological refractory period; a human heart cannot beat faster than 400 BPM in ordinary states).
- `height = 0.5 std` (ignores low-voltage baseline fluctuations).

### Feature Formulation

```
R-Peak Detection (Lead II) ──▶ Calculate R-R Intervals (ms)
                                     │
         ┌───────────────────────────┴───────────────────────────┐
         ▼                                                       ▼
Statistical Features (Waveform Shape)                   Temporal HRV Features (Rhythm)
• mean, std, max, min                                   • heart_rate = (peaks / duration) * 60
• energy = ∑(x_i)^2                                     • rr_mean = average(R-R_i)
• zero_crossings                                        • hrv (sdnn) = std(R-R_i)
• skewness (asymmetry)                                  • rmssd = √(mean(diff(R-R_i)^2))
• kurtosis (peakedness)                                 • pnn50 = % of diff(R-R_i) > 50ms
```

### Pearson Correlation & Feature Redundancy Analysis
A Pearson correlation matrix was computed across the 14 core numeric features to evaluate redundancies:
1. **Redundancy of Heart Rate**: `heart_rate` and `peak_count` exhibit a correlation coefficient of exactly $r = 1.00$ because heart rate is a direct linear scale of the peak count over 10 seconds. In training, LightGBM automatically selects the split with the highest information gain (usually `rr_mean` or `peak_count`) and ignores the redundant duplicate.
2. **Inverse Relationship**: `rr_mean` and `heart_rate` show a strong negative correlation ($r = -0.93$), which reflects the physiological law that faster heart rates shorten the interval between beats.
3. **Orthogonality of HRV**: Heart Rate Variability (HRV/SDNN) shows extremely low correlation with average heart rate ($r = -0.21$) and mean R-R interval ($r = 0.10$). This is highly significant: it proves that HRV captures an independent physiological dimension (the regularity of the spacing, rather than the average speed), which serves as the primary separator between Normal rhythms and pathological irregularities (Atrial Fibrillation).

## 6. Feature Extraction Branch B: Deep CNN Embeddings (Wavelet CWT)

Instead of manually coding rules to detect complex morphological changes (like ST-segment elevation or inverted T-waves), we use deep transfer learning.

### Step 1: Continuous Wavelet Transform (CWT)
The 1D time-series signals of the three selected leads (I, II, V5) are mapped into a 2D time-frequency space using a Complex Morlet wavelet (`cmor1.5-1.0`) across 128 scale frequencies:

$$CWT(a, b) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{|a|}} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x(t) \psi^*\left(\frac{t - b}{a}\right) dt$$

This transform outputs a 2D matrix representing how the energy of different frequency bands fluctuates across the 10-second recording window.

### Step 2: The 3-Channel Image Trick
1. Compute the CWT matrix for Lead I, Lead II, and Lead V5.
2. Normalize each matrix to range $[0, 255]$ and convert to a grayscale grid.
3. Stack the three grayscale grids as the **Red, Green, and Blue channels** of a single RGB image.
4. Resize to **$380 \times 380$ pixels** (the standard input dimensions of EfficientNet-B4).

This stack allows the Convolutional Neural Network to analyze multi-lead alignment as color/texture patterns, matching how 2D convolutions process images.

```
Lead I   CWT (128x5000) ──▶ Red Channel   ──┐
Lead II  CWT (128x5000) ──▶ Green Channel ──┼──▶ RGB Image (380x380x3) ──▶ EfficientNet-B4
Lead V5  CWT (128x5000) ──▶ Blue Channel  ──┘
```

### Step 3: Feature Extraction (EfficientNet-B4)
We load an **EfficientNet-B4** backbone pre-trained on ImageNet. 
- The classification head is removed and replaced with a flat `Identity()` layer.
- The stacked scalogram is passed through the network.
- We capture the output of the **Global Average Pooling** layer, yielding a dense, rich **1792-dimensional embedding vector** that represents the visual structures of the ECG.
- Weights are frozen during feature extraction to avoid distortion and maintain reproducible, deterministic feature representations.

## 7. Hybrid Feature Fusion & PCA Compression

### Feature Concatenation
For each patient, the feature branches are combined:
$$\mathbf{X}_{raw} = [\mathbf{X}_{handcrafted} \ (17) \ \parallel \ \mathbf{X}_{deep} \ (1792) \ \parallel \ \mathbf{X}_{demographics} \ (2)] \implies 1811 \text{ total features}$$

### The Curse of Dimensionality & Overfitting Mitigations
Training a model with 1,811 features on a dataset of only 1,000 samples would result in massive overfitting (the model would memorize noise patterns rather than generalizable features). Two steps prevent this:

1. **StandardScaler**: The fused vector is normalized to zero-mean and unit variance. This prevents high-magnitude features (like signal energy) from drowning out low-magnitude but highly discriminative features (like HRV).
2. **Principal Component Analysis (PCA)**: The 1,792 deep feature columns are compressed down to the **30 most informative principal components** (eigenvectors of the covariance matrix). These 30 components capture over 88% of the total variance in the deep embeddings, providing a low-dimensional representation that represents the dominant visual patterns of the scalograms.

The final compressed vector has **49 features** (17 handcrafted, 30 PCA deep components, age, sex), which is perfectly scaled for tree splits.

## 8. Classifier Model Optimization (LightGBM)

The classifier is built using **LightGBM** (Light Gradient Boosting Machine), chosen for its exceptional handling of tabular inputs, speed, and resistance to overfitting via depth limits.

### Hyperparameters & Settings
*   `n_estimators`: 150
*   `learning_rate`: 0.05
*   `max_depth`: 6 (prevents deep trees from memorizing training splits)
*   `class_weight`: `'balanced'` (mitigates class imbalance)
*   `random_state`: 42

### Class Imbalance Mitigation Strategy
The Georgia/South-Asian cohort has a significant class skew:
- **Arrhythmia (Class 1)**: ~60% (majority class)
- **Other/Unknown (Class 2)**: ~23%
- **Normal (Class 0)**: ~17%

If evaluated purely on raw accuracy, a naive model that predicts Arrhythmia for every patient would achieve a 60% score while being clinically useless. To mitigate this:
1. **Weighted Loss**: We apply balanced class weighting, penalizing errors on the minority class (Normal) more than the majority class.
2. **Macro-F1 Focus**: Models were selected based on **Macro-F1** (which calculates the F1-score for each class independently and averages them with equal weight).
3. **Stratified Partitioning**: The 80/20 train-test split and 3-fold cross-validation folds were strictly stratified to ensure the class ratios are identically represented in both training and test partitions.

## 9. Prototype vs. Production Model Evolution

The project progressed through two distinct development generations, moving from a baseline prototype to a highly optimized deep hybrid architecture:

### 1. Prototype Phase (EfficientNet-B0 Backbone)
- **Scale**: Trained on a 300-record subset.
- **Parameters**: Scalogram images sized at $224 \times 224$ pixels, generating 1,280-dimensional embeddings.
- **Performance**:
  - Test Accuracy: **88.3%**
  - Macro-F1: **80.0%**
  - AUC (weighted): **94.9%**

### 2. Production Phase (EfficientNet-B4 Backbone)
- **Scale**: Trained on the full 1,000-record cohort.
- **Parameters**: Scalogram images sized at $380 \times 380$ pixels, generating 1,792-dimensional embeddings.
- **Performance**:
  - Test Accuracy: **92.0%** (+3.7% improvement)
  - Macro-F1: **89.5%** (+9.5% improvement)
  - AUC: **97.1%** (+2.2% improvement)
  - **Arrhythmia Recall**: **95.6%** (only 4.4% false negatives)

### Strategy Benchmark Comparison (1000-Record Cohort)

To prove the efficacy of transfer learning, we benchmarked the different feature strategies:

| Strategy | Features | CV Accuracy | Test Accuracy | Test Macro-F1 | AUC |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **Handcrafted Only** | 17 | **92.9%** | 91.0% | 87.9% | 96.0% |
| **EfficientNet-B4 Only** | 1792 | 88.4% | **92.0%** | **89.5%** | **97.1%** |
| **Hybrid (Fused)** | 1809 | 88.3% | 92.0% | 89.3% | 96.5% |

*Conclusion*: While handcrafted features alone achieved a high cross-validation accuracy, the **EfficientNet-B4 Deep Features** strategy yielded the best generalization on the test set, achieving the highest **Macro-F1 (89.5%)** and **AUC (97.1%)**.

## 10. Inference-Time Vitals Risk Assessment Logic

The raw prediction from the LightGBM classifier (probabilities for Class 0, 1, and 2) is post-processed using a set of clinical override rules to assign a patient risk level:

```
                            Class Prediction
                                   │
         ┌─────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────┐
         ▼                         ▼                         ▼
      Normal                   Arrhythmia                  Other
         │                         │                         │
         ▼                         ▼                         ▼
      LOW Risk             Confidence ≥ 85%?          Confidence ≥ 80%?
                           ┌───────┴───────┐          ┌───────┴───────┐
                          Yes              No        Yes              No
                           ▼               ▼          ▼               ▼
                       HIGH Risk      MEDIUM Risk  MEDIUM Risk     LOW Risk
         │                         │                         │
         └─────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────┘
                                   │
                                   ▼
                       Heart Rate (HR) Vitals?
                       • HR < 40 or HR > 120 BPM
                                   │
                                   ├─────────────────────────┐
                                   ▼                         ▼
                                  Yes                       No
                                   │                         │
                                   ▼                         ▼
                            Escalate Risk              Maintain Risk
                           (LOW -> MEDIUM)
                           (MEDIUM -> HIGH)
```

### Justification of Vitals Overrides
Even if the model predicts "Normal" with high confidence, a heart rate below 40 BPM (severe bradycardia) or above 120 BPM (severe tachycardia) represents a significant physiological abnormality. This safety override escalates the risk level by one step, ensuring that critical vital instabilities are flagged for immediate review.

## 11. Explainable AI Layer (Gemma LLM / Rule-based)

To transition from raw classification outputs to actionable decision support, the system incorporates an **Explainable AI (XAI) Layer**. 

1. **Feature Attribution**: The system tracks the clinical features (such as heart rate, HRV/SDNN, RMSSD, and age) that deviate most from the healthy population averages.
2. **Natural Language Explanations**: A local **Gemma LLM** model (or a structured rule-based generator when computing resources are constrained) translates these attributions into a natural language summary.

*Example Generated Output:*
> **Clinical Assessment**: The model predicts Arrhythmia with a confidence of 98.61%, representing a HIGH clinical risk. This is supported by a significant elevation in Heart Rate (114.0 BPM) and an abnormal Heart Rate Variability (HRV of 49.78 ms). Immediate review of the 12-lead ECG is recommended to rule out tachyarrhythmia.

| **`model_metadata_efficientnet_b4.json`** | Metadata JSON | 1 KB | Records the training history, metrics, and integrity hashes. |

### SHA256 Integrity Safeguard
To prevent deployment errors where a model file from one version (e.g., B4) is run with a feature list from another (e.g., B0), we compute the SHA256 hash of the feature list columns and save it in the metadata:
```json
"feature_list_hash": "0ad456858783af12d659d35b54ef43fe4a6c062cfbe530c51c7b9eab4934a71a"
```
During inference, the system recomputes the SHA256 hash of the loaded feature list. If it does not match the metadata hash, the system aborts execution. This ensures complete reliability and pipeline security.
